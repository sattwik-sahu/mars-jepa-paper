\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

The proposed framework described in \cref{sec:methodology} was evaluated on the MMLSv2 dataset~\cite{mmlsv2_2026}, a multimodal Martian landslide segmentation benchmark consisting of co-registered $128 \times 128$ GeoTIFF tiles. Each sample contains seven heterogeneous sensing channels: Thermal Inertia (THEMIS, $\sim$\mppx{100}), Slope maps derived from DEM data, Digital Elevation Models (MOLA/HRSC, $\sim$\mppx{200}), Grayscale imagery (CTX, $\sim$\mppx{6}), and a three-channel RGB basemap (Viking mosaics, $\sim$\mppx{232}).

The publicly available split of the dataset contains 465 labeled training samples and 66 labeled validation samples. Because the sensing modalities have vastly different numerical ranges, we apply global channel-wise normalization prior to training. Specifically, the pixel values in each of the seven channels are standardized to zero mean and unit variance using the global statistics (mean and standard deviation) computed for that respective channel across the entire training dataset. This standard practice ensures numerical stability during optimization and minimizes distributional discrepancies between the heterogeneous sensors.

All training and inference were performed on a single NVIDIA RTX A4500 GPU with \qty{20}{\giga\byte} VRAM.
