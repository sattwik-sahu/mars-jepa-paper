Our SS-JEPA + UPerNet model achieves $0.865$ mIoU, defining the Pareto frontier in \cref{fig:perf-size-comp}. It outperforms standard supervised baselines like U-Net++~\cite{zhou2019unetplusplus}, PSPNet~\cite{zhao2017pyramidsceneparsingnetwork}, DeepLabV3+~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1802-02611}, and SegFormer~\cite{xie2021segformer} which plateau between $0.81$ and $0.83$, proving fully supervised training from scratch cannot fully exploit complex cross-modal correlations. At just 20M parameters, our single-model framework is 5.6$\times$ smaller than the 113M parameter DualSwinV2 5-fold ensemble~\cite{kabir2026dualswinfusionsegmultimodalmartianlandslide}, providing highly competitive performance, trailing the latter by just \( 0.2\% \) mIoU. It also beats the previous best single-model baseline, DualSwinV2-S~\cite{kabir2026dualswinfusionsegmultimodalmartianlandslide}, by $3.7\%$ mIoU and the 33M parameter TinySwin + FPN by $2.8\%$ mIoU. Transitioning from I-JEPA to SS-JEPA yields a $15.6\%$ parameter reduction alongside a $2.8\%$ mIoU gain, confirming that our spatio-spectral masking strategy internalizes inter-modal structures more effectively than spatial-only SSL.

Class-specific metrics in \cref{tab:all-vs-ours} validate this architectural reliability. SS-JEPA secures the highest background IoU ($0.910$), reflecting superior noise rejection, alongside a foreground IoU of $0.820$ with balanced precision-recall. These results demonstrate that our SS-JEPA captures richer geological semantics than standard supervised alternatives. By minimizing inference overhead while preserving robust representations, this single-pass framework replaces heavy, computationally expensive ensembles, posing as a suitable candidate for the low-power hardware typical of planetary exploration platforms and Martian orbiters.
