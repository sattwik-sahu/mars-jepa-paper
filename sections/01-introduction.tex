\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The martian surface is frequented by landslides, which shape the planet's geomorphology, simultaneously posing a major threat to current and future robotic and human assets deployed on the surface.

Autonomous planetary exploration and mapping, specifically on Mars, has generated vast amounts of remote sensing data, massively accelerating our understanding of extra-terrestrial environments. Recent datasets like MMLSv2~\cite{mmlsv2_2026} have shifted the field from monochromatic analysis to mutlispectral imagery, stacking bands like visible (RGB), digital elevation model (DEM), slope and thermal inertia together. However, landslides, a frequent phenomenon on Mars, is characterized by mass movement of debris across large areas. This necessitates autonomous geomorphological feature mapping and landslide detection, which is critical for identifying geological shifts and safeguarding robotic and human assets deployed on the surface of the planet.

Traditional fully supervised learning methods are designed with inductive biases to effectively capture spatial relationships, but struggle to learn spectral correlations in multispectral imagery, leading to the learned representations being inadequate for semantic segmentation tasks on such multimodal data, thus relying heavily on sheer model size to overcome the performance barrier~\cite{kabir2026dualswinfusionsegmultimodalmartianlandslide}. While existing self-supervised methods~\cite{Ericsson_2022} can leverage unlabelled data to learn spectral correlations, their reliance on generative approaches like pixel reconstruction from masked inputs~\cite{he2022masked} renders them computationally prohibitive for high-dimensional multispectral data.

Different spectral bands carry independent but correlated information about a spatial patch. Applying masking independently across spectra and spatial patches forces the model to learn structural as well as physical dependencies. With this insight, we introduce SS-JEPA, a novel self-supervised framework designed for learning meaningful semantic representations from multispectral imagery, based on the joint embedding predictive architecture. Implementing channel-wise tokenization and the aforementioned spatial-spectral masking strategy, the model learns inter-modal correlations across spatial patches and spectra, through a latent predictive objective. Our SS-JEPA encoder, paired with a UPerNet~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1807-10221} segmentation decoder head, achieves performance on par with state-of-the-art fully-supervised ensemble models \( 5.6\times \) larger (20M vs 113M), and beats baseline models with JEPA backbones trained with spatial-only masking, specifically I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023selfsupervisedlearningimagesjointembedding}, by \( 2.8\% \) mIoU.

Our specific contributions include:
\vspace{-0.75em}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,label=\roman*)]
	\item We propose SS-JEPA, a joint-embedding predictive architecture designed for multispectral image encoding via a novel spatial-spectral masking strategy
	\item We demonstrate that forcing the model to learn cross-spectral latent correlations yields representations that capture rich geological and physical semantics, validated qualitatively through latent visualizations
	\item We achieve state-of-the-art single-model performance on the MMLSv2 dataset, significantly outperforming supervised baselines while demonstrating extreme parameter and compute efficiency.
\end{enumerate}
