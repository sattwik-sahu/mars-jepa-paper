\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The martian surface is frequented by landslides, which shape the planet's geomorphology, simultaneously posing a major threat to current and future robotic and human assets deployed on the surface.

Autonomous planetary exploration and mapping, specifically on Mars, has generated vast amounts of remote sensing data, massively accelerating our understanding of extra-terrestrial environments. Analysis of the Martian terrain highlights the frequent occurence of landslides, characterized the mass movement of debris across large areas. This necessitates autonomous geomorphological feature mapping and landslide detection, which is critical for identifying geological shifts and safeguarding robotic and human assets deployed on the surface of the planet.
Recent datasets like MMLSv2~\cite{mmlsv2_2026} have shifted the field from monochromatic analysis to multispectral imagery, stacking bands like visible (RGB), digital elevation model (DEM), slope and thermal inertia together.

Traditional fully supervised learning methods are designed with inductive biases to effectively capture spatial relationships, but struggle to learn spectral correlations in multispectral imagery. This results in the learned representations failing to capture the multimodal dependencies present in such data, leading to heavy reliance on larger model sizes to overcome the performance barrier~\cite{kabir2026dualswinfusionsegmultimodalmartianlandslide} on landslide detection tasks.

Meanwhile, self-supervised methods~\cite{Ericsson_2022} can be used to learn rich semantic features, but the usage of generative approaches such as pixel reconstruction from masked inputs~\cite{he2022masked} limits their computational efficiency for high-dimensional multispectral data. The introduction of the joint embedding predictive architecture (JEPA) aims to solve this by modeling representation learning as a predictive task in the latent space, overcoming the computational complexity associated with pixel-level reconstruction. However, specific adaptations of this framework addressing the challenge of learning semantic representations from multispectral data remain underexplored.

Applying masking independently across spectra and spatial patches forces the model to learn structural as well as physical dependencies. With this insight, we introduce SS-JEPA, a novel self-supervised framework designed for learning meaningful semantic representations from multispectral imagery, based on the joint embedding predictive architecture. Implementing channel-wise tokenization and the aforementioned spatial-spectral masking strategy, the model learns inter-modal correlations across spatial patches and spectra, through a latent predictive objective. Our SS-JEPA encoder, paired with a UPerNet~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1807-10221} segmentation decoder head, achieves performance on par with state-of-the-art fully-supervised ensemble models \( 5.6\times \) larger (20M vs 113M), and beats baseline models with JEPA backbones trained with spatial-only masking, specifically I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023selfsupervisedlearningimagesjointembedding}, by \( 2.8\% \) mIoU.

Our primary insight is that for a given spatial region, different spectral bands carry independent but correlated information. To learn rich semantic representations for multispectral data, the model must develop an understanding of these inter-modal relationships. To this end, we introduce Spatio-Spectral JEPA (SS-JEPA), an adaptation of the JEPA framework to learn rich semantic representations from multispectral imagery. Implementing tokenization and masking across both, spatial regions and spectra, the model learns spatial as well as spectral relationships in the data through a latent prediction objective. Our SS-JEPA encoder, paired with a UPerNet~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1807-10221} segmentation decoder head, achieves performance on par with state-of-the-art fully supervised ensemble models \( 5.6\times \) larger (20M vs 113M), and beats baseline models with JEPA backbones trained with spatial-only masking, specifically I-JEPA~\cite{assran2023selfsupervisedlearningimagesjointembedding}, by \( 2.8\% \) mIoU. Further, qualitative latent visualizations reveal the representations learned by our SS-JEPA to be much richer in semantic information than those learned by the I-JEPA baseline encoder.

Our specific contributions include:
\vspace{-0.75em}
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,label=\roman*)]
	\item We propose SS-JEPA, a joint-embedding predictive architecture designed for multispectral image encoding via a novel spatial-spectral masking strategy
	\item We demonstrate that forcing the model to learn cross-spectral latent correlations yields representations that capture rich geological and physical semantics, validated qualitatively through latent visualizations
	\item We achieve state-of-the-art single-model performance on the MMLSv2 dataset, significantly outperforming supervised baselines while demonstrating extreme parameter and compute efficiency.
\end{enumerate}
