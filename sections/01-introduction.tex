\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Autonomous planetary exploration and mapping, specifically on Mars, has generated vast amounts of multi-sensor remote sensing data, thanks to the massive acceleration in space technology in recent years. The martian surface is frequented by landslides, which shape the planet's geomorphology, simultaneously posing a major threat to current and future robotic and human assets deployed on the surface.

Autonomous planetary exploration and mapping, specifically on Mars, has generated vast amounts of remote sensing data, massively accelerating our understanding of extra-terrestrial environments, with recent datasets like MMLSv2~\cite{mmlsv2_2026} shifting the field from monochromatic analysis to mutlispectral imagery, stacking bands like visible (RGB), digital elevation model (DEM), slope and thermal inertia together. However, landslides, a frequent phenomenon on Mars, is characterized by mass movement of debris across large areas. This necessitates autonomous geomorphological feature mapping and landslide detection, which is critical for identifying geological shifts and safeguarding robotic and human assets deployed on the surface of the planet.
