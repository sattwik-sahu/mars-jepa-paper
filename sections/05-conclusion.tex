\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
In this work, we introduced a Spatio-Spectral Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (SS-JEPA) specifically designed for the challenges of multimodal Martian landslide segmentation. By shifting from the traditional generative pixel-reconstruction paradigm to a predictive latent-representation framework, our approach effectively overcomes the issues of sensor noise and data scarcity inherent in orbital Martian imagery.

Our primary contribution, the spatio-spectral masking strategy which allows the model to learn deep physical correlations between heterogeneous modalities, such as topography, thermal inertia, and visual textures, without requiring extensive human-labeled data. By tokenizing channels independently and utilizing \textit{factorized learned embeddings}, the SS-JEPA encoder produces a context-rich feature pyramid that significantly enhances the performance of downstream segmentation decoders.The quantitative analysis demonstrates that the proposed model achieves an mIoU of \(0.865\) using only \(20\)M parameters.
This performance is nearly identical to the current state-of-the-art DualSwinV2 ensemble~\cite{kabir2026dualswinfusionsegmultimodalmartianlandslide}, which requires approximately \(113\) million parameters along with a \(5\)-fold cross-validation strategy. Consequently, the proposed approach provides an approximate \(\sim 5.6 \times\) reduction in model size without any significant degradation in segmentation performance.

The learned representations further highlight the importance of cross-modal interactions in multispectral planetary imagery, demonstrating how geological structures can manifest differently across spectral bands while still preserving meaningful latent relationships. Extending this framework toward larger and more diverse planetary datasets could enable the development of more generalized foundation models for autonomous geomorphological understanding and planetary surface analysis.
