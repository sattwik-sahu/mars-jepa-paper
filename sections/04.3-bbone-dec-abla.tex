\subsection{Backbone and Decoder Compatibility}
\label{sec:backbone-dec-compat}

The comparative analysis of segmentation performance across different encoder backbones is presented in \cref{tab:ssl-enc-seg}. Our results demonstrate that the proposed SS-JEPA architecture, when paired with a UPerNet~\cite{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-1807-10221} decoder, achieves the highest performance with an mIoU of $0.865$. This represents a $2.8\%$ improvement over the best I-JEPA configuration, which utilizes a SegFormer~\cite{xie2021segformer} head to reach $0.837$ mIoU. The consistent performance gain observed with the SS-JEPA backbone validates the superior effectiveness of the spatio-spectral masking strategy over the standard spatial-only masking approach.

The observed performance gap is primarily attributed to the differences in the predictive constraints imposed during self-supervised pre-training. In the multispectral I-JEPA implementation, the application of an identical spatial mask across all \( n_c \) channels restricts the encoder to learning representations based on spatial proximity. In this configuration, if a region is masked, the model lacks information across all spectral modalities for that location, forcing the predictor to rely solely on surrounding spatial context. In contrast, the channel-wise masking in SS-JEPA introduces an inter-modal predictive task. Because patches are masked independently per channel, the encoder must learn to reconstruct latent features in one spectral band (\eg, thermal inertia) using visible context from other bands (\eg, digital elevation model or visible bands). This forces the model to internalize the underlying physical and geological correlations between different Martian surface modalities, resulting in more descriptive semantic embeddings, resulting in improved segmentation performance.
